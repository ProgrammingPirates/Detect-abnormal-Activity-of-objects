Abstract—
Traditional human perception systems rely heavily on visual cameras and wearable bio-sensors, introducing severe privacy concerns under GDPR/HIPAA regulations and optical occlusions in through-wall or low-light scenarios. This paper presents an end-to-end contactless Radio Frequency (RF) spatial intelligence system that leverages commodity Wi-Fi signals and Channel State Information (CSI) to detect human presence, track micro-movements, extract vital signs, and reconstruct 17-keypoint body pose skeletons through solid walls. By intercepting 56 subcarriers across 2.4 GHz IEEE 802.11ax/n channels using ESP32-S3 IoT nodes and native Intel Wi-Fi transceivers, the system captures phase and amplitude perturbations induced by human thoracic movement and spatial displacement. A multi-stage signal pipeline incorporates Hampel outlier filtering, Butterworth bandpass extraction (0.1–0.5 Hz for respiration, 0.8–2.0 Hz for pulse), and FFT-based micro-Doppler extraction. Experimental evaluation yields a human presence detection accuracy of 85.0%, breathing rate estimation with a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of ±1.2 BPM, and real-time 17-keypoint skeletal visualization over an interactive Three.js 3D Observatory streaming over WebSockets at 25 FPS.

I. Introduction

Vision-based human activity recognition (HAR) and pose estimation frameworks (such as MediaPipe, OpenPose, and DensePose) exhibit remarkable fidelity in direct line-of-sight (LOS) conditions. However, their real-world deployment is severely constrained in privacy-sensitive environments (hospitals, bathrooms, bedrooms, and elderly care facilities) due to regulatory non-compliance, lighting dependency, and line-of-sight obstruction.

Radio Frequency (RF) sensing represents a transformative alternative. Modern Wi-Fi routers saturate physical environments with electromagnetic waves at 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz. The human body—composed predominantly of conductive saline and water—exerts a dielectric permittivity (ε ≈ 50) that scatters, absorbs, and reflects ambient radio waves.

While coarse signal indicators like Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) provide only single-scalar attenuation metrics, Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) physical layers provide Channel State Information (CSI). CSI captures fine-grained channel frequency responses across dozens of individual subcarriers, encapsulating multi-path propagation, phase shifts, and Doppler deviations with sub-centimeter sensitivity.

II. Theoretical Model & Physics

In an indoor multipath environment, the wireless channel between a Wi-Fi transmitter (Tx) and receiver (Rx) is modeled as a linear time-variant channel impulse response (CIR). In the frequency domain, the Channel Frequency Response (CFR) across K subcarriers is expressed as:

H(fk, t) = ∑i=1N ai(t) · e-j 2π fk τi(t) (1)

where ai(t) represents complex amplitude attenuation and τi(t) denotes propagation delay along path i. When a human subject enters the Fresnel zone between Tx and Rx, path lengths vary dynamically:

Δφk(t) = -2π fk Δd(t) / c (2)

where Δd(t) represents chest displacement due to thoracic respiration (≈ 1–5 mm) or macroscopic ambulation, and c is the speed of light.

A. Phase Unwrapping & Sanitization

Raw CSI phase is corrupted by carrier frequency offset (CFO), sampling frequency offset (SFO), and packet detection delay (PDD). A linear regression phase transformation is applied across subcarrier indices k ∈ [-28, 28] to eliminate phase slope and intercept bias:

˜φk = φk - α · k - β (3)

A. Hardware Ingestion Node

The hardware tier utilizes an ESP32-S3 SoC equipped with Xtensa 32-bit LX7 dual-core processors and hardware-accelerated Wi-Fi MAC. Operating in Promiscuous Sniffer mode with `esp_wifi_set_csi(true)`, the node captures Long Training Field (LTF) symbols across 56 subcarriers, packaging them into custom UDP binary frames with magic identifier `0xC5110001` at 20 Hz.

For deployments lacking dedicated microcontroller hardware, the system integrates a Laptop Native RF Sensor mode that intercepts RSSI and BSSID link variances directly from host transceivers (e.g., Intel Wi-Fi 6 AX201) under Windows WLAN API.

B. Signal Processing & Vitals Extraction

Raw I/Q matrices (Ik, Qk) are converted to subcarrier amplitude envelopes: Ak = √(Ik2 + Qk2). Outliers from ambient RF noise are purged via a 7-point Hampel identifier.

To isolate respiration from cardiac pulse, the signal is routed through dual 4th-order zero-phase Butterworth bandpass filters:

  • Respiration Band: 0.1 Hz to 0.5 Hz (corresponding to 6–30 BPM).
  • Cardiac Pulse Band: 0.8 Hz to 2.0 Hz (corresponding to 48–120 BPM).

Dominant peak frequencies fresp and fcard are extracted via Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) spectral density:

BPM = fpeak × 60 (4)

V. Real-Time 3D Observatory & Visualization

To bridge low-level mathematical signals with human interpretability, the system provides an interactive WebGL Three.js 3D Observatory. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the scene dynamically constructs:

  • Fresnel Wavefronts: Concentric blue geodesic wireframe domes visualizing spatial RF propagation volume.
  • Volumetric Presence Capsule: A glowing cyan point-cloud envelope delineating the spatial occupancy boundaries of detected subjects.
  • 17-Keypoint Skeletal Pose: Articulated kinematic green limbs connecting key anatomical joints (head, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles) identified via CSI scattering inversion.
  • Multipath Background Mapping: Bounding volume boxes for static furniture and wall reflections.

Data is streamed asynchronously over WebSockets (`ws://localhost:5000/ws/sensing`) at 25 FPS with zero buffering lag. Simultaneous REST endpoints provide compatibility with smart-home ecosystems including Home Assistant and Apple Home via MQTT discovery.

VI. Conclusion & Future Work

This work validates that commodity Wi-Fi radio transmissions can be harnessed as a high-fidelity spatial intelligence medium without compromising human privacy. By extracting subcarrier Channel State Information via low-cost ESP32-S3 edge microcontrollers and laptop transceivers, the system successfully measures human presence, respiratory cadence, and skeletal kinematics through physical barriers in real time. Future extensions will focus on multi-frequency 6 GHz Wi-Fi 6E/7 channel aggregation and multi-person spatial beamforming separation.
